🤔 What is the Game of Life?

It's a famous maths game invented by John Conway in 1970. Nobody controls the cells — they follow simple counting rules, and amazing creatures appear all by themselves!

  • 🔢 Every square looks at its 8 neighbours (up, down, left, right and the corners) and counts how many are alive.
  • 🐣 An empty square with exactly 3 living neighbours grows a new cell — like having enough friends for a party.
  • 💪 A living cell with 2 or 3 neighbours is happy and survives.
  • 💤 With fewer than 2 it gets lonely, with more than 3 it gets too crowded — either way it disappears.
  • 🚀 Try stamping a Glider and watch it fly, or the Glider Factory to build a spaceship machine!
  • 🧪 Then visit the Rule Lab and change the numbers — can you invent a rule that makes mazes? Coral? Total chaos?
  • 🔍 Not sure why a cell lived or died? Pick the 🔍 Inspect tool and tap it — the game shows its neighbour count and reads the rule out to you.
  • 🔮 Ready for a real challenge? Try Predict the Next Generation — that's exactly what a computer does, one square at a time.
  • 🏅 Some shapes never change (still lifes), some flip back and forth forever (oscillators) — find them and earn Discovery badges!
